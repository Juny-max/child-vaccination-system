CHAPTER TWO
LITERATURE REVIEW
Digital Solution for Tracking and Managing Child Vaccination Records Across Multiple Healthcare Branches

2.1 Introduction
This chapter offers a comprehensive review of the literature concerning digital health systems, tools for electronic vaccination tracking, immunization management platforms, and the coordination of healthcare records across multiple branches. This review aims to establish a solid theoretical base for a digital solution designed to track and manage children's vaccination records throughout various healthcare facilities. It examines the current state of knowledge, identifies gaps, and underscores technological opportunities pertinent to immunization management. Detailed discussions are included on issues such as the fragmentation of healthcare data, manual record-keeping, low adherence to vaccination schedules, and limited interoperability among different branches.
Worldwide, childhood immunization is recognized as one of the most effective public health measures. Nonetheless, problems such as inadequate documentation, poor data access, and ineffective tracking systems continue to impede successful vaccination initiatives. In several developing nations, including Ghana, child vaccination records are often maintained manually in health record books (weighing cards). These physical documents can easily be lost, damaged, or contain incomplete information, leading to missed vaccination appointments and inaccuracies in health data reporting. Introducing a digital solution could modernize the management of immunizations, minimize inefficiencies, and enhance the continuity of care among various healthcare facilities.
Thus, this chapter concentrates on examining: the evolution of immunization record systems, theoretical frameworks that support the adoption of digital health, current solutions for vaccination tracking, challenges in managing data across multiple branches, applicable technologies, and research gaps that rationalize the need for the proposed system.


2.2 History of Vaccination and Record-Keeping
Vaccination has been practiced for centuries, evolving from early inoculation methods in ancient societies to the advanced immunization programs we have today. Initial record-keeping started with handwritten logs, which later transitioned into standardized immunization cards that are now used worldwide. Although these paper-based systems were popular, they presented significant challenges, such as poor readability, storage difficulties, and limited access across different healthcare sectors.
As we entered the early 21st century, the necessity for electronic immunization registries (EIRs) became apparent. Countries like the United States, the United Kingdom, and South Korea created centralized electronic systems to monitor vaccination statuses on a national scale. These systems improved forecasting, reporting, and tracking of immunization trends. However, many low- and middle-income countries face hurdles in adoption due to issues related to infrastructure, finances, and workforce capacity.
In Ghana, the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) has seen success, but record management is still largely manual. Implementing a digital vaccination management solution across multiple branches would mark a significant advancement in modernization efforts.

2.3 Theoretical Foundations
Several theoretical frameworks guide the design, implementation, and adoption of digital health solutions such as online vaccination tracking systems. These frameworks help explain how users perceive and use new technologies, and they also highlight the factors that influence successful implementation within healthcare environments.
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), developed by Davis (1989), is one of the most widely used theories for predicting user adoption of new technologies. TAM proposes that two key factors determine whether users will accept and effectively use a system:
1. Perceived Usefulness (PU): The extent to which a user believes that using the system will improve job performance.
In the context of a vaccination tracking system, healthcare workers are more likely to adopt the platform if they perceive it as improving their efficiency, such as reducing paperwork, preventing missed vaccinations, or improving record accuracy.
2. Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU): The degree to which a user believes that using the system will require minimal effort.
For busy nurses, clinicians, and administrative staff, a vaccination system should be easy to navigate, require minimal training, and integrate smoothly into existing workflows. If the system is seen as complicated or time-consuming, adoption rates are likely to decline.
TAM emphasizes that when users find a system both useful and easy to use, they are more likely to develop a positive attitude toward it and continue using it regularly. Thus, successful digital vaccination platforms must be designed with user experience, simplicity, and practical benefits in mind.
Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)
The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), proposed by Venkatesh et al. (2003), expands TAM by incorporating additional determinants that influence user behavior. UTAUT identifies four major constructs affecting technology adoption:
1. Performance Expectancy: Similar to perceived usefulness, it refers to how strongly users believe that the technology will help them achieve better outcomes. For example, health workers will adopt a vaccination system if it helps track patient immunizations more accurately and improves the delivery of health services.
2. Effort Expectancy: This refers to how simple and user-friendly the system appears. A digital system with a clear interface, automation features, and minimal data entry effort increases the likelihood of widespread adoption.
3. Social Influence: UTAUT recognizes that the opinions of people around them also influence individuals. In healthcare settings, if administrators, supervisors, or peer professionals advocate for the vaccination system, workers are more likely to use it. Strong support from senior hospital staff or Ministry of Health officials can significantly boost adoption rates.
4. Facilitating Conditions: This refers to the organizational and technical environment supporting system use. Availability of ICT infrastructure, reliable internet connectivity, computers, training, and technical support all play major roles in determining the successful adoption of digital vaccination tracking systems.
UTAUT therefore provides a broader perspective by considering social and environmental elements, making it particularly relevant to healthcare systems where technology adoption is influenced not only by personal preference but also by policy frameworks, professional culture, and institutional readiness.
Together, TAM and UTAUT offer useful theoretical foundations for understanding how digital child vaccination systems can be successfully introduced, accepted, and sustained within healthcare organizations.

Health Information Systems Success Model (HISSM)
This model emphasizes system quality, information quality, service quality, user satisfaction, and overall system impact.

RE-AIM Framework (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance)
This model is relevant for evaluating digital vaccination systems in terms of their long-term sustainability and scalability across healthcare branches.

2. 4 Types of Digital Vaccination Tracking Systems
A variety of digital systems have been developed to improve the recording, monitoring, and management of child immunization data. Each system offers different features depending on the technological infrastructure, health sector needs, and scale of operation. Below are the major types of digital vaccination tracking systems commonly used in healthcare:
1. Electronic Immunization Registries (EIRs)
Electronic Immunization Registries are large centralized digital databases used by governments or regional health authorities to store vaccination records for children at a national or district level. These systems enable health officials to:
* Maintain comprehensive and standardized immunization data
* Identify children who have missed vaccinations
* Monitor vaccination coverage and trends over time
* Generate reports for health planning and policy development
* Predict vaccine demand and manage supply effectively
EIRs play a key role in expanding public health surveillance and ensuring that national immunization programs remain data-driven, efficient, and transparent.
2. Mobile Vaccination Apps 
Mobile vaccination applications are smartphone-based tools designed for parents and caregivers. These apps make it easy for guardians to monitor their child's vaccination process independently. They often include features such as:
* Digital versions of vaccination cards
* Automatic reminders for upcoming or overdue vaccines
* Health alerts and educational content
* Options to download or print immunization proof
* Push notifications to ensure no appointment is missed
Mobile apps increase parental awareness and help reduce reliance on physical vaccine cards that may be lost or damaged.
3. Clinic-Based Vaccination Management Systems
These systems are installed directly within healthcare facilities such as hospitals, maternity wards, community clinics, and immunization centers. Their primary function is to support healthcare workers during clinical service delivery by:
* Digitizing vaccination data at the point of care
* Providing quick access to patient immunization history
* Reducing manual paperwork and filing burdens
* Generating clinic-level performance and coverage reports
While they are effective within a single facility, they may not support real-time data sharing across multiple locations unless integrated with a broader network. This can limit continuity of care when patients move between different health centers.
4. Cloud-Based Multi-Branch Vaccination Solutions
Cloud-based systems store vaccination data online, allowing multiple healthcare branches to access and update records in real time. These systems ensure that a child's immunization information follows them regardless of where they receive care. Advantages include:
* Centralized data accessible from all participating facilities
* Improved accuracy and reduced duplication of patient records
* Faster and more consistent access to vaccination history
* Enhanced data analytics and decision-making across the organization
* Seamless coordination among healthcare centers, outreach clinics, and mobile units
These systems are ideal for hospitals or healthcare organizations operating multiple service points. The proposed solution in this study is based on this model due to its ability to improve efficiency, transparency, and continuity of patient care.
5. Smart Card and QR-Based Vaccination Records
Smart card and QR-code systems provide each child with a small physical card embedded with digital identifiers. When scanned, healthcare workers can retrieve vaccination information quickly. Benefits include:
* Reduced reliance on paper-based immunization records
* Fast access to digital records, even in areas with limited internet connectivity
* Improved verification of patient identity and vaccination history
* Minimized data loss due to misplaced or damaged cards
These solutions are particularly useful in rural or low-resource environments where internet access or advanced data-sharing infrastructure may be limited.

2.5 Existing Child Vaccination Systems
A number of digital child vaccination systems have been developed and implemented globally to enhance the efficiency, traceability, and coverage of immunization programs. These systems are designed to replace traditional paper-based methods with modern digital tools that improve data accuracy, real-time monitoring, and service delivery. Despite their varying levels of sophistication, these systems share the common goal of improving public health outcomes by ensuring that children receive the correct vaccines at the appropriate times.
One of the widely recognized solutions is the WHO Smart Immunization Platform, which provides a centralized digital platform for immunization data collection and analysis. This system enables health workers to digitally record vaccination activities using mobile devices and upload the information into a centralized database. The platform supports real-time data analytics, helping health ministries and decision-makers identify coverage gaps, forecast vaccine demand, and develop targeted interventions. By improving data visibility and reducing delays associated with paper-based reporting, the WHO Smart Immunization Platform has contributed to better monitoring and planning in immunization programs.
Another major system in widespread use is the DHIS2 Tracker, a module of the District Health Information System 2. This system is adopted by several African and Asian countries, including Ghana, to manage both individual-level and aggregated health information. DHIS2 Tracker allows health facilities to enter vaccination data during service delivery and supports reporting across administrative levels. The system enhances accountability and enables governments to assess immunization performance across regions. However, in many countries, including Ghana, DHIS2 is primarily configured to capture aggregated vaccination totals rather than detailed, patient-level vaccination histories. This limits its ability to support child-specific follow-up across multiple facilities.
In addition, mVaccination tools have gained popularity as cost-effective solutions, especially in low-resource settings. These tools typically use SMS, mobile applications, and automated messaging services to send reminders to parents and caregivers about upcoming vaccination schedules, missed doses, and educational information about the importance of immunization. By leveraging the widespread availability of mobile phones, these tools help reduce vaccination dropouts, increase community awareness, and strengthen compliance with national immunization schedules. In regions where digital infrastructure is limited, mVaccination solutions serve as a practical bridge between healthcare facilities and caregivers.
Despite their successes, existing child vaccination systems face several challenges that limit their effectiveness. One major concern is limited interoperability, where digital systems often operate in silos and cannot integrate or exchange data with other platforms. This leads to fragmented health information and restricts centralized visibility of vaccination records. Additionally, many systems lack multi-branch synchronization, meaning that a child's vaccination record may not be accessible outside the facility where it was originally recorded. As a result, children who move between clinics may experience service gaps or repeated vaccinations.
Another limitation is the continued reliance on manual data entry, even in digital programs. This increases the risk of errors, delays in reporting, and inconsistent record quality. In many developing regions, challenges such as inadequate power supply, poor network connectivity, insufficient computer hardware, and low digital literacy further restrict the use of digital vaccination systems. These infrastructure limitations, especially in rural areas, create obstacles to the full digitization of vaccination services.
In the context of Ghana, platforms such as DHIS2 are widely used in public health reporting but remain largely aggregate-based, focusing on summarizing vaccination totals rather than maintaining detailed, child-level histories. This makes comprehensive tracking across multiple branches difficult and prevents health workers from quickly accessing a child's full immunization record when they attend a different facility. Consequently, the system does not provide the level of continuity and real-time monitoring necessary for efficient child vaccination management, highlighting the need for more integrated, branch-synchronized digital systems.

2.6 Benefits of Digital Child Vaccination Tracking Systems
The implementation of a digital multi-branch child vaccination tracking system offers numerous advantages to healthcare providers, caregivers, and national health authorities. By replacing fragmented paper-based processes with centralized digital solutions, such systems enhance efficiency, improve decision-making, and strengthen the delivery of immunization services across geographically dispersed health facilities.
One of the key benefits is improved continuity of care. Digital vaccination records stored in a centralized or cloud-based database can be accessed from any participating branch or health facility. This ensures that children who relocate, visit different clinics, or attend outreach programs have their complete medical and vaccination history available, regardless of where care is received. This continuity minimizes the risk of duplicated vaccinations, delays in service delivery, and incomplete immunization schedules.
Another major benefit is the prevention of missed appointments. Digital systems often integrate SMS alerts, push notifications, or in-app reminders to notify parents and caregivers about upcoming vaccination dates. These automated reminders improve adherence to national vaccination schedules by helping families remember due dates, follow-up visits, or catch-up schedules when doses are missed. As a result, vaccination dropout rates are reduced, and more children receive timely immunization.
Digital systems also contribute significantly to data accuracy. Automated data entry, structured digital forms, and real-time validation reduce the common errors associated with manual or paper-based documentation. Mistakes such as unreadable handwriting, misplaced documents, or delayed data transfer are minimized. This leads to more reliable patient records, stronger accountability, and higher-quality health data that can be used for monitoring and evaluation.
In addition, digital tracking platforms support efficient health reporting. Administrators and public health officials can easily generate reports on immunization coverage, stock levels, regional performance, and facility compliance. These reports can be produced in minutes rather than days, enabling faster decision-making and more responsive program management. National immunization programs benefit from accurate, timely, and standardized data that support regulatory reporting and international monitoring frameworks.
Finally, digital systems enhance evidence-based decision-making at both operational and managerial levels. Real-time data visibility allows healthcare leaders to identify trends such as vaccine shortages, outreach needs, underserved communities, or low-coverage zones. This information can guide the allocation of resources, planning of vaccination campaigns, and development of targeted interventions. Predictive analytics can also help forecast vaccine consumption, preventing stockouts and ensuring continuous availability of essential doses.
Overall, digital child vaccination tracking systems strengthen the quality, timeliness, and efficiency of immunization services while ensuring that children remain fully protected throughout their vaccination journey.

2.7 Challenges of Digital Child Vaccination Systems
While digital child vaccination tracking systems offer significant benefits, their implementation and sustained operation face a range of challenges. These challenges can affect the efficiency, reliability, and acceptance of such systems, particularly in low-resource settings.
Infrastructural Limitations are among the most significant barriers. Many health facilities, especially in rural or remote areas, experience poor internet connectivity, unreliable network coverage, and inconsistent electricity supply. These issues can disrupt the functioning of digital systems, prevent real-time data synchronization, and hinder timely access to vaccination records. Without stable infrastructure, the effectiveness of digital tracking systems is compromised, and reliance on paper-based backup systems may remain necessary.
Digital Literacy among healthcare workers is another critical challenge. Not all staff may be familiar with computers, mobile applications, or cloud-based systems. This can lead to improper use, data entry errors, and underutilization of the system's capabilities. Effective training programs and ongoing technical support are essential to ensure that all personnel can navigate digital platforms efficiently and confidently.
Data Security Concerns also pose a significant challenge. Vaccination records contain sensitive personal information, including the identity of children and their caregivers. Digital systems must comply with national and international data protection regulations to prevent unauthorized access, breaches, or misuse of health data. Failure to implement robust security measures can undermine public trust and expose healthcare providers to legal and ethical risks.
Cost Constraints further complicate implementation. Developing, deploying, and maintaining a digital vaccination system requires financial investment in hardware, software, internet connectivity, training, and technical support. For many low-resource health facilities, these costs can be prohibitive, leading to delayed adoption or partial implementation that limits system effectiveness.
Resistance to Change is a human and organizational challenge. Healthcare workers who are accustomed to manual, paper-based processes may be reluctant to adopt new digital methods. Resistance can stem from fear of increased workload, lack of familiarity, or skepticism about the benefits of technology. Overcoming this requires clear communication of advantages, adequate training, and involvement of staff in the system design and rollout process to foster acceptance and ownership.
In conclusion, while digital child vaccination systems have the potential to transform immunization programs, addressing these infrastructural, human, financial, and security challenges is crucial for successful adoption and sustainable operation. Strategic planning, resource allocation, and stakeholder engagement are key to overcoming these obstacles and maximizing the impact of digital vaccination tracking.

2.8 Current System
In Ghana, as in many developing countries, child vaccination records are predominantly managed through traditional manual methods. These systems typically rely on paper-based child health record books, manual registers, and clinic-specific entries that are not synchronized across multiple branches or health facilities. While these methods have been in use for decades and provide a basic level of record-keeping, they are increasingly inadequate for the demands of modern healthcare management, particularly in the context of national immunization programs.
The reliance on paper-based record books means that each child's vaccination history is physically stored at a single facility, making it difficult to access records if the child moves to a different clinic or region. Similarly, manual registers kept by healthcare workers are prone to human error, including misrecorded dates, missed entries, and inconsistent data formatting. Clinic-based entries that are not networked or synchronized prevent the seamless sharing of vaccination information across facilities, creating gaps in continuity of care.
These manual processes result in several significant challenges. First, there is a high risk of lost or incomplete records, particularly when books are misplaced, damaged, or not updated promptly. Second, the lack of centralized tracking leads to duplication of vaccinations, as children may receive the same dose more than once at different clinics due to missing records. Third, it is difficult to track children who move between branches, which affects the timely delivery of vaccines and increases the likelihood of missed doses. Finally, generating accurate reports for health administrators and policymakers becomes labor-intensive and time-consuming, often requiring manual aggregation of data from multiple sources. This delays critical decision-making and limits the ability to respond effectively to gaps in immunization coverage.
Overall, the current manual system in Ghana underscores the need for a digital, multi-branch vaccination tracking solution that can centralize records, improve data accuracy, enable continuity of care, and facilitate efficient reporting for national immunization programs.
2.9 Proposed System
The proposed system is designed to address the limitations of current child vaccination tracking methods by providing a comprehensive digital platform capable of managing vaccination records across multiple healthcare branches. This system leverages modern technology to ensure accuracy, accessibility, and efficiency, while also improving the overall quality of immunization services. By integrating real-time data management with user-friendly features, the platform aims to enhance continuity of care, reduce missed vaccinations, and provide actionable insights for healthcare administrators.
A central component of the system is a user-friendly interface for health workers. The interface is designed to simplify the process of entering and updating vaccination records, reducing the likelihood of errors and minimizing the time required for routine administrative tasks. Clear navigation, structured data entry forms, and intuitive workflows ensure that even healthcare personnel with limited digital experience can efficiently use the system.
The centralized cloud database forms the backbone of the proposed system. This cloud-based repository allows vaccination data to be stored securely and accessed in real time by any authorized branch. With centralized storage, health workers can view complete immunization histories of children regardless of where they received prior vaccinations. This ensures seamless continuity of care and prevents issues such as duplicate vaccinations or missed doses when children move between facilities.
To support timely vaccination, the system includes automated reminder notifications. Parents and caregivers receive SMS alerts or app-based reminders about upcoming vaccination dates, missed doses, or follow-up schedules. These reminders improve adherence to vaccination timelines and reduce dropout rates, contributing to higher coverage and healthier communities.
Each branch is equipped with branch-level dashboards that display key metrics, including vaccination trends, performance statistics, and coverage rates. These dashboards provide actionable insights for facility managers, enabling them to monitor service delivery, identify gaps, and optimize resource allocation at the branch level.
The system is designed with interoperability in mind, allowing integration with existing national health systems such as DHIS2. This ensures that data collected at the branch level can contribute to national reporting, planning, and policy-making, while avoiding data silos and duplication.
Security and access control are critical features of the proposed system. Role-based authentication ensures that only authorized personnel can access sensitive child health records, protecting privacy and maintaining compliance with national data protection regulations. Audit trails and encryption further strengthen data security and accountability.
Finally, the system offers offline capabilities to address challenges in areas with limited internet connectivity. Health workers can continue to enter vaccination data locally, and the system automatically synchronizes records with the central database once connectivity is restored. This feature ensures uninterrupted service delivery and reliable data capture even in low-resource or rural settings.
In summary, the proposed digital child vaccination system combines accessibility, security, and interoperability with practical tools for health workers and caregivers. By streamlining record management, improving communication, and enabling real-time monitoring, the system aims to enhance immunization coverage and support efficient, data-driven healthcare delivery across multiple branches.

2.10 Conclusion
This literature review underscores the pressing need for a digital solution to manage child vaccination records in multi-branch healthcare settings. Current systems, both globally and in Ghana, often fail to provide seamless access to vaccination histories across different facilities, resulting in fragmented records, missed vaccinations, and inefficiencies in service delivery. Paper-based registers, manual record-keeping, and clinic-specific documentation limit the ability of healthcare providers to maintain comprehensive, up-to-date immunization histories, particularly for children who move between branches or regions.
The proposed system addresses these challenges by integrating a centralized cloud-based database, user-friendly interfaces, automated reminders, and offline functionality to ensure that vaccination data is accessible, accurate, and complete. By facilitating real-time access across multiple branches, the system ensures continuity of care, reduces the risk of duplication or missed doses, and improves adherence to national immunization schedules. Furthermore, the system's interoperability with existing platforms such as DHIS2 allows for seamless data integration, supporting broader public health planning and reporting.
This review also emphasizes the importance of theoretical frameworks such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), and RE-AIM (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance). These models provide critical guidance for understanding user acceptance, predicting system adoption, and ensuring the sustainability of digital health interventions. Applying these frameworks ensures that the system is designed not only for technological efficiency but also for effective adoption by healthcare workers and caregivers, thereby maximizing its real-world impact.
In conclusion, implementing a multi-branch digital child vaccination tracking system offers significant benefits for healthcare delivery in Ghana and similar contexts. It enhances data accuracy, strengthens health service management, promotes timely and complete immunization, and provides actionable insights for national public health programs. By addressing both technological and human factors, such a system represents a crucial step toward more coordinated, efficient, and equitable vaccination services, ultimately contributing to improved child health outcomes and stronger national immunization efforts.
